Consumer Intelligence

Amazon Product Customer Intelligence

67,325 real Amazon Electronics reviews (1999–2014) from UCSD's 5-core dataset. Customer sentiment analysis, rating distribution, review length vs. helpfulness correlation, seasonal patterns, and brand performance ranking.

What This Means

Amazon's Electronics shoppers are generally satisfied (4.22★ average) but the real signal is in the long tail: detailed negative reviews get 91% helpfulness because buyers trust specificity. Q4 is review season — volume doubles. If you're selling electronics, prioritize thorough product descriptions to preempt the detailed complaints that dominate helpfulness.

Market Intelligence

Google Search Trends Analysis

14 search topics tracked over 5 years (2021–2026) via live pytrends API. Multi-category time-series, YoY growth analysis, US regional choropleth, and correlation matrices.

What This Means

AI isn't just trending — it's structurally diverging from every other search category. The 71% YoY growth is sustained, not viral. Inflation interest peaked in 2022-2023 and is now cooling (good signal for monetary policy watchers). Mental health searches are rising steadily (+63%), suggesting a durable shift in public attention, not a news cycle blip.

Figure Gallery

Selected analysis outputs with plain-language insights

💡 Five-star reviews are the default — but the signal is in the tail.59.5% of all reviews are 5★, which means negative reviews (especially 1–2★) are disproportionately informative for product improvement because they break the positivity bias.
💡 Review volume tracks marketplace maturity.Monthly reviews peaked in 2013 at 25,000+, tracking Amazon's Electronics category growth. The post-2012 slowdown isn't declining interest — it's market saturation.
💡 Angry customers write 16% more — and people read them.1-star reviews average 642 characters vs. 553 for 5-star. The anger-verbs-and-details effect makes negative reviews longer and, paradoxically, more trusted.
💡 Length is the strongest predictor of helpfulness.Long reviews (500+ chars) achieve 91% helpfulness vs. 78% for short ones. If you want your review to help others, write more — specificity beats brevity in trust signals.
💡 Q4 doubles everything — plan for the holiday surge.Review volume spikes in November-December across all rating categories. If you're analyzing product performance, normalize by season or you'll misread Q1 as decline.
💡 The headline numbers for stakeholder decks.67,325 reviews · 4.22★ average · 27,832 unique products · 1999-2014 span. Use this as the single slide that answers "what's the size of this dataset?"
💡 Netflix is a movie platform that also has TV.Movies dominate at 69.6% (6,131 titles) vs. TV Shows at 30.4% (2,676). But TV shows have higher average ratings — the volume leader isn't the quality leader.
💡 Drama and Comedy are safe bets by volume.They lead the catalog by title count. But for audience satisfaction per dollar spent, look at Thriller and Documentary — smaller catalogs, higher ratings, less competition.
💡 Popular ≠ Good (correlation ≈ 0.05).There's almost no relationship between how many people watch something and how highly they rate it. Marketing can drive views; quality drives retention. They're independent levers.
💡 ~35% of the catalog is "Good" — the upgrade opportunity.Only ~15% scores Excellent. The bulk sits in Good (35%) and Average (30%). For a content strategy, upgrading Good → Excellent has higher ROI than adding more Good.
💡 One dashboard, four decisions.KPI cards, content mix pie, top genres bar, rating distribution, upcoming pipeline, and trailer view. Designed for the stakeholder who wants the answer in 30 seconds.
💡 The sunburst shows where quality lives by genre.Radial breakdown: Drama dominates by volume, but Documentary and Thriller occupy larger quality-adjusted slices. Use this for "where should we invest?" conversations.
💡 AI is in its own category now — it's not "tech" anymore.Worldwide search interest shows AI's divergence from every other topic since 2023. This is structural growth, not a hype cycle.
💡 Three stories in one chart: AI boom, inflation peak, mental health shift.AI +70.7% YoY, inflation peaked at +68.9% and is cooling, mental health rising +62.5% — a durable public attention shift, not a news blip.
💡 California and New York drive tech-topic search — but the Midwest is catching up.US regional choropleth with state-level interest scores. Target geo campaigns using actual search behavior, not population density.
💡 Spot emerging trends before they hit headlines.Breakout alerts flag rising related queries by parent keyword. "AI" spawns "AI art," "AI detector," "AI jobs" — early signals of where attention is migrating.
